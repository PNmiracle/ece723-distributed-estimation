From the rectangle to transcript entropy

\[ \nabla_\theta\ln p_\theta(T\mid R=r)=\class{tA}{\textstyle\sum_i a_i(T)},\qquad a_i(t)=\mathbb E_\theta[A_i\mid Z_i\in E_i(t)] \]

III-D · 1 / 2

Overlap breaks independence: separate shared from private

Three machines read from one pool of six samples. S₁ = {1, 3, 6}, S₂ = {2, 3, 6}, S₃ = {3, 4, 5}. Machines 1 and 2 both read \(X_3\) and \(X_6\), so \(Z_1,Z_2\) are dependent: Lemma 3 does not apply.
Dashed circles mark where each machine read a sample; they stay after the samples move.
\[ \mathcal G=\{a:\textstyle\sum_i\mathbf 1\{a\in S_i\}\ge2\}=\{3,6\} \]
\[ \mathcal V_1=\{1\},\ \mathcal V_2=\{2\},\ \mathcal V_3=\{4,5\};\qquad r=|\mathcal G|=2,\ \ q=\max_i|\mathcal V_i|=2 \]
\[ \mathcal L_\theta(G,V_1,\dots,V_n)=P_\theta^{\otimes r}\otimes\textstyle\bigotimes_i P_\theta^{\otimes q_i},\qquad 0\le q_i\le m \]
Circle every index read twice or more: G = {3, 6}. What is left is private: V1 = {1}, V2 = {2}, V3 = {4, 5}. Shared values G and private blocks V_i use disjoint indices, so they are independent. This restores the hypothesis of Lemma 3, once G is fixed.

This is the step worth doing slowly. d/m is the error of one machine with m samples. Saying the error relative to that scale is x, the ln m from the budget and the ln m from the normalization cancel. The surplus s may be negative here; the positive part comes later.

III-E · Step 6

Remove the \((B+1)^2\) correction: two regimes

Two regimes in the \((n,m)\) plane
\[ D\gtrsim\frac{d}{m(1+s_+)+\bar r} \]
\[ \class{tG}{(B+1)^2}=(nk+1)^2\le(k+1)^2n^2\le\class{tM}{(k+1)^2m} \]

Case A: many local samples, so the Bernstein term is at most a constant times m. Case B: the surplus grows linearly in n, so the refinement term is already smaller than d/(nm), which III-A proved. III-E · Backup optional

Gaussian means: explicit constants via Lambert \(W\)

\[ \mathbb E_\Theta\,\mathrm{tr}\,J_{T,\mathcal S,R}\le d\bar r+2m\,h_T \]
\[ \frac{d^2}{D}\le d\bar r+2m\,h_T+4\pi^2d \]
\[ t\le\frac{\bar r}{m}+\frac{2h_T}{d}+\frac{4\pi^2}{m} \]
\[ t+\ln t\le z:=2s\ln2+\frac{\bar r}{m}+\ln\frac{\pi e^3}{2}+\frac{4\pi^2}{m} \]
\[ te^t\le e^z\ \Rightarrow\ t\le W_0(e^z),\qquad W_0(y)e^{W_0(y)}=y \]
\[ D\ \ge\ \frac{d}{m\,W_0\!\Big(\frac{\pi e^3}{2}\exp\big[2s\ln2+\bar r/m+4\pi^2/m\big]\Big)} \]
Same proof, no hidden constant. Lambert W only does the last algebraic inversion; the statistics came from the rectangle, the additive shared term, and the entropy budget. This is a non-asymptotic Bayes bound and therefore also bounds Gaussian minimax risk.
