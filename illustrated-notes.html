III-C · Independent private blocks

Part I · Independent Private Blocks

第一部分：III-C · Independent Private Blocks

What can the server infer?

1. What problem are we solving?

Imagine ten machines, each with a hundred temperature readings, estimating the same mean θ.

Each machine holds its own hundred observations, with no duplicates across machines.

They can exchange only a few bits, but they can interact.

For example:

  • Machine 1 sends a bit.

  • Machine 2 reads it and adapts its encoding rule.

  • Machine 3 uses both earlier messages to decide what to send.

  • The server uses the complete transcript T to estimate θ.

This raises a question.

With many observations but few bits, how much statistical information about θ survives communication?

Section III-C bounds that information from above.

Overlap makes local inputs dependent. First solve the independent case; Section III-D will recover it conditionally.

1. 这一节到底要解决什么问题？

先想象：有 10 台机器，每台收集了 100 条温度观测，要共同估计真实平均温度 θ。

每台机器有自己的 100 条数据，任何两台机器之间都没有重复观测。

但是每台机器只能发送少量 bits，而且机器之间还可以交互。

例如：

  • Machine 1 先发送一个 bit。

  • Machine 2 看到这个 bit 后，调整自己的编码方式。

  • Machine 3 根据前两台机器的消息决定发送什么。

  • 最终中央服务器根据完整记录 T 估计 θ。

现在问题来了：

机器内部有大量样本，但能发送的 bits 很少。经过通信以后，还能保留多少关于 θ 的统计信息？

Section III-C 的任务就是证明这个信息量的上界。

它暂时不考虑重叠数据，因为重叠会让不同机器的数据产生依赖。先搞定独立数据，再去 III-D 解决重叠问题。

2. Equation (146) · Define the local blocks

Zi=(Xi,1,…,Xi,qi),0≤qi≤q,

Xi,j At machine i, observation number j

Lemma 3 and its role

3. Lemma 3 · The target bound

Equation (147) of the paper

tr⁡JT,R(θ)≤4vqHθ(T∣R)+2b2KB,

Notation used here

Symbol

Interpretation
T Complete transcript of all messages
R All public and private random seeds used by the protocol
JT,R(θ) Fisher information matrix of the transcript and seeds together
tr Trace: sum of the diagonal entries
Hθ(T∣R) Transcript entropy at the given parameter, conditional on the seeds
v Variance proxy for the score
b Bernstein scale controlling large score deviations
KB Budget-dependent bound on the second moment of surprisal for budget B

In full, KB is

𝔼ln⁡21pT(T)≤KB,KB:=(Bln⁡2)2+2Bln⁡2+2.

How should we read the inequality?

The left side measures information available to the observer.

The right side places a ceiling on it.

Under the stronger sub-Gaussian assumption b=0:

tr⁡JT,R(θ)≤2vqHθ(T∣R)

Since at most B bits are transmitted:

Hθ(T∣R)≤Bln⁡2

Consequently:

tr⁡JT,R(θ)≤2vqBln⁡2

This follows directly from (148); it has no separate equation number in the paper.

What does (147) accomplish? Alice and Bob can exchange messages repeatedly. Given the seeds, Hθ(T∣R) measures the remaining transcript uncertainty, and tr⁡JT,R(θ) measures how sensitive its distribution is to a parameter change. Independent private blocks and the score concentration assumption bound the latter by 4vqHθ(T∣R)+2b2KB for any admissible interaction. Use (147) in the general case. When b=0, the sharper score bound gives (148), improving the coefficient from 4vq to 2vq.

Where is it used? In III-D, fix the shared reading X3. The remaining private samples satisfy the independence requirement of (147). Equation (184) applies the bound to the information still carried by the conversation. Adding the shared information yields (174).

Why does this continue to hold across multiple interactive rounds?

The rest of III-C proves it.

A transcript as a rectangle

4. Product Structure of a Transcript Event

The event that a given transcript occurs has a product structure.

Equations (149)–(157)

4.1 Why examine the transcript event?

The machines can interact.

Alice changes Bob’s rule, and Bob's reply can affect Alice's next message.

The machines' actions therefore appear intertwined.

Read the conversation backward. Alice and Bob each hold a number card. Alice checks whether hers exceeds five. On yes, Bob checks whether his exceeds three; on no, his threshold is seven.

Freeze the transcript at yes, yes. The first yes establishes both Alice’s constraint and Bob’s threshold. The second tells us Bob’s card exceeds three.

Try swapping cards. The pair (6,4) produces this transcript. Replace six by a hundred, four by twenty, or both: the answers stay the same. Every admissible Alice card pairs with every admissible Bob card. On axes for Alice and Bob, the possible pairs fill the region to the right of X=5 and above Y=3.

This is the rectangle property.

4.2 A two-machine example

Alice holds X and Bob holds Y.

First round:

T1=𝟏{X>5}

In round two, Bob chooses his rule using Alice’s message:

T2={𝟏{Y>3},T1=1𝟏{Y>7},T1=0

Fix the final transcript:

t=(1,1)

This requires:

X>5,Y>3

Every pair (X,Y) satisfying both conditions generates that same transcript.

Its event is therefore:

{T=(1,1)}={X>5}×{Y>3}

Such a Cartesian product of local input sets is called a rectangle.

Why does this matter?

Alice’s constraint involves only X and Bob’s only Y.

We can therefore compute their local probabilities separately.

4.3 Equation (149) · A checklist for each machine

Ei(t;r):=⋂1≤ℓ≤Liℓ(t,r)=i{zi:gℓ,r(zi,tℓ−1)=tℓ}.

Read the intersection one round at a time.

Symbol Meaning
t=(t1,…,tL) One fixed complete transcript
L≤B Actual number of bits in this transcript
r Fixed realization of the random seeds
iℓ(t,r) Sender at round ℓ
gℓ,r Deterministic encoding rule at round ℓ
tℓ−1 Public message history before this round
Ei(t;r) All constraints that machine i must satisfy

The intersection ⋂ means all the conditions must hold.

Suppose Alice speaks three times.

  • Sending one the first time requires X>5.

  • Sending zero next requires X<9.

  • Sending one the third time requires X>7.

Her checklist is:

E1(t;r)={X>5}∩{X<9}∩{X>7}

It simplifies to:

E1(t;r)={7<X<9}

Equation (149) collects all the local constraints imposed on one machine along the transcript.

4.4 Equation (150) · The rectangle identity

{T=t}=⋂i=1n{Zi∈Ei(t;r)},conditional on R=r.

Above Y = 3 and to the right of X = 5, every input produces two yeses.

In words:

The complete transcript equals t precisely when every local input passes its checklist.

The next sender and stopping rule must follow from the public history and allowed randomness. They cannot encode an additional unreported private decision.

This precludes an extra joint constraint across machines.

4.5 Equations (151)–(152) · Factor the transcript probability

Define:

pi(t):=ℙθ(Zi∈Ei(t)),p(t):=ℙθ(T=t∣R=r),ℓi(t):=ln⁡1pi(t).

Here pi(t) is the probability that machine i passes its checklist; ℓi(t) is the corresponding surprisal.

Since the inputs are independent:

p(t)=∏i=1npi(t)

Suppose Alice passes with probability 0.2 and Bob with probability 0.3:

p(t)=0.2×0.3=0.06

Take negative logarithms:

∑i=1nℓi(t)=ln⁡1p(t)

Labels (152a) and (152b) split the two parts of the paper’s equation (152) for study.

The point of this identity:

Taking logs turns a product into a sum; averaging then turns local contributions into transcript entropy.

4.6 Equation (153) · Why are the conditioned inputs still independent?

ℙθ(Zi∈Ci∀i∣T=t,R=r)=∏i=1nℙθ(Zi∈Ci∩Ei(t))∏i=1npi(t)=∏i=1nℙθ(Zi∈Ci∣Zi∈Ei(t)).

Which pairs remain? Alice draws uniformly from {6,8,10}, Bob from {2,4,6}, independently. Suppose the transcript event is exactly 7<X<9 and Y>3. Alice can hold only {8}; Bob can hold {4,6}. Of nine equally likely pairs, exactly (8,4) and (8,6) remain. Bob has a six with probability 1/2. Conditioning just on Y>3 gives the same probability 1/2.

Given the entire transcript, one of two equally likely pairs has Y = 6. Given only Y > 3, three of six pairs have Y = 6. Thus 1/2 = 3/6. Before either condition, the probability is 1/3. Here the two local constraints are necessary and sufficient for the fixed transcript.

The identity says:

Conditioning independent blocks on separate local constraints preserves their independence.

Why does this matter?

Next we will separate the machines’ Fisher information contributions.

4.7 Equations (154)–(157) · Why do cross terms cancel?

Define the block score at machine i:

Ai=∑a=1qiξθ(Xi,a)

where

ξθ(X)=∇θlog⁡pθ(X)

is the score function.

A score measures how the log likelihood changes with the parameter. For independent observations the block score is the sum of their scores.

Also define:

ai(t)=𝔼θ[Ai∣T=t,R=r]

This is the conditional expectation of the machine’s raw block score given the transcript.

By the rectangle property:

ai(t)=𝔼θ[Ai∣Zi∈Ei(t)].

To compute machine i’s conditional score, its own local event suffices.

Hence for distinct machines i≠j:

𝔼θ[AiAj⊤∣T=t,R=r]=ai(t)aj(t)⊤.

This follows from conditional independence given the complete transcript.

Now average over transcripts:

𝔼θ[ai(T)aj(T)⊤∣R=r]=𝔼θ[𝔼θ[AiAj⊤∣T,R=r]∣R=r]=𝔼θ[AiAj⊤∣R=r]=0.

When is the cross term zero? Two machines each report one independent Bernoulli (θ) observation. At θ=1/2 the score is −2 or +2. Each report reveals its input, so the conditional mean score equals the observed score. In transcript order 00,01,10,11, the products are 4,−4,−4,4. The product for 00 is four; each transcript has probability 1/4, giving an average of (4−4−4+4)/4=0.

Why zero?

Independent original block scores have mean zero:

𝔼θ[Ai]=0

Consequently:

𝔼θ[AiAj⊤]=𝔼θ[Ai]𝔼θ[Aj]⊤=0

A distinction to emphasize in the talk:

A particular transcript can give a nonzero cross term. Cancellation occurs after averaging over all transcripts.

Section recap

The argument can be stated in one sentence:

Even though the communication protocol is interactive, fixing the complete transcript gives a rectangular event. This preserves conditional independence and allows the cross terms to cancel.

A fixed interactive transcript gives a rectangle. The conditioned inputs remain independent, so cross terms cancel on averaging.

4. III-C 第一小节：Product Structure of a Transcript Event

中文：通信记录事件的乘积结构。

对应论文 (149)–(157)

4.1 为什么要研究这一小节？

因为机器之间可以交互。

Alice 发什么会影响 Bob 发什么；Bob 的消息可能又影响 Alice 下一轮发什么。

看起来，所有机器的通信行为都纠缠在一起。

把对话倒着读。 Alice 和 Bob 各拿一张数字卡。Alice 先判断自己的数是否大于 5。她答“是”，Bob 就判断自己的数是否大于 3；她答“否”，Bob 就改用门槛 7。

现在黑板上已经写着“是、是”。第一声“是”告诉我们 Alice 的数大于 5，也确定了 Bob 用的是门槛 3；第二声“是”告诉我们 Bob 的数大于 3。

试着换卡。 (6,4) 能产生这段对话。把 Alice 的 6 换成 100，或把 Bob 的 4 换成 20，甚至两张一起换，对话仍是“是、是”。两人的卡只要各自满足条件，就能任意搭配。横轴画 Alice 的数，纵轴画 Bob 的数，所有可行搭配恰好填满 X=5 右侧、Y=3 上方的区域。

这就是 Rectangle Property（矩形性质）。

4.2 从一个两台机器的例子开始

Alice 拥有 X，Bob 拥有 Y。

第一轮：

T1=𝟏{X>5}

第二轮，Bob 根据 Alice 发出的内容决定自己的规则：

T2={𝟏{Y>3},T1=1𝟏{Y>7},T1=0

假设我们固定最终通信记录：

t=(1,1)

这要求：

X>5,Y>3

满足这些条件的任何一组数据 (X,Y)，都会产生同一条通信记录。

这条记录对应的是：

{T=(1,1)}={X>5}×{Y>3}

我们把二维输入空间里的这种乘积集合叫 Rectangle。

为什么重要？

因为 Alice 的条件只涉及 X，Bob 的条件只涉及 Y。

因此可以分别计算对应的概率。

4.3 公式 (149)：为每台机器建立一份检查清单

Ei(t;r):=⋂1≤ℓ≤Liℓ(t,r)=i{zi:gℓ,r(zi,tℓ−1)=tℓ}.

这条看起来复杂，但含义很简单。

例如，Alice 一共发送三次：

  • 第一次发送 1，要求 X>5。

  • 第二次发送 0，要求 X<9。

  • 第三次发送 1，要求 X>7。

所以 (149) 只是把一台机器在多轮通信中的所有局部限制合在一起。

4.4 公式 (150)：最重要的矩形等式

{T=t}=⋂i=1n{Zi∈Ei(t;r)},conditional on R=r.

人话翻译：

整条通信记录等于 t，当且仅当每台机器的本地数据都通过了自己的检查清单。

这里有一个前提：谁发送下一位、什么时候停止，都只能由公开历史和允许的公共随机性决定，不能偷偷依赖尚未传输的私有数据。

这保证了没有额外的跨机器联合约束。

4.5 公式 (151)–(152)：把通信概率拆成乘积

作者定义：

pi(t):=ℙθ(Zi∈Ei(t)),p(t):=ℙθ(T=t∣R=r),ℓi(t):=ln⁡1pi(t).

其中 pi(t) 是第 i 台机器通过局部检查的概率，ℓi(t) 是对应的自信息。

由于不同机器的数据相互独立：

p(t)=∏i=1npi(t)

假设 Alice 满足条件的概率是 0.2，Bob 是 0.3：

p(t)=0.2×0.3=0.06

再取负对数：

∑i=1nℓi(t)=ln⁡1p(t)

这里我把论文同一个公式 (152) 的两部分标记为 (152a)、(152b)，方便你学习。

它的重要性是：

乘积概率取对数后变成求和，这让不同机器的数据贡献最终可以汇总成通信熵。

4.6 公式 (153)：给定通信记录后，数据为什么还独立？

ℙθ(Zi∈Ci∀i∣T=t,R=r)=∏i=1nℙθ(Zi∈Ci∩Ei(t))∏i=1npi(t)=∏i=1nℙθ(Zi∈Ci∣Zi∈Ei(t)).

剩下哪几对卡？ Alice 从 {6,8,10} 抽一张，Bob 从 {2,4,6} 抽一张，两人独立、等概率抽取。假设固定记录要求 7<X<9 且 Y>3。Alice 只剩 {8}，Bob 剩 {4,6}，所以原来的九对卡只剩 (8,4) 和 (8,6)。在这条记录下，Bob 拿到 6 的概率就是 1/2；只看 Bob 自己通过的条件 Y>3，也得到 1/2。

固定种子后，假设这两个条件恰好刻画整条记录。给定完整记录，两个等可能的组合中有一个满足 Y = 6；只给定 Y > 3，六个等可能的组合中有三个满足 Y = 6。因此 1/2 = 3/6。完全不加条件时，概率是 1/3。

这条式子说：

如果原始数据块独立，而且我们只对每个块分别施加局部条件，那么条件化之后，各个数据块仍然独立。

为什么重要？

因为下一步要把所有机器的 Fisher Information 分开计算。

4.7 公式 (154)–(157)：为什么交叉项可以消失？

先定义第 i 台机器的 Block Score：

Ai=∑a=1qiξθ(Xi,a)

其中：

ξθ(X)=∇θlog⁡pθ(X)

叫 Score Function（得分函数）。

人话： Score 描述一条样本的对数概率对未知参数的变化有多敏感；Block Score 是整个数据块的 Score 之和。

再定义：

ai(t)=𝔼θ[Ai∣T=t,R=r]

这是已知消息以后，对机器原始 Block Score 的条件期望。

由矩形结构：

ai(t)=𝔼θ[Ai∣Zi∈Ei(t)].

意思是：为了计算第 i 台机器的条件 Score，我们只需要知道本机的局部条件。

于是对于不同的机器 i≠j：

𝔼θ[AiAj⊤∣T=t,R=r]=ai(t)aj(t)⊤.

这来自给定完整通信记录后的条件独立性。

再对所有可能的通信记录求平均：

𝔼θ[ai(T)aj(T)⊤∣R=r]=𝔼θ[𝔼θ[AiAj⊤∣T,R=r]∣R=r]=𝔼θ[AiAj⊤∣R=r]=0.

交叉项是哪一步变成零的？ 两台机器各观察一个独立的 Bernoulli(θ) 样本，并如实发送 0 或 1。在 θ=1/2 处，单样本得分为 −2 或 +2。记录完全揭示了样本，所以条件平均得分就等于实际得分。按记录 00,01,10,11 的顺序，两机得分的乘积依次为 4,−4,−4,4。固定 00 时乘积是 4；四条记录各占 1/4，平均才得到 (4−4−4+4)/4=0。

为什么等于 0？

因为原始独立块的 Score 具有零均值：

𝔼θ[Ai]=0

因此：

𝔼θ[AiAj⊤]=𝔼θ[Ai]𝔼θ[Aj]⊤=0

注意一个非常容易讲错的细节：

不是每一条固定通信记录的交叉项都为零，而是对所有记录取平均之后，交叉项才抵消。

本小节总结

这部分所有数学细节，只服务于一句话：

Even though the communication protocol is interactive, fixing the complete transcript gives a rectangular event. This preserves conditional independence and allows the cross terms to cancel.

即使通信协议是交互式的，固定完整通信记录后仍然具有矩形结构，使条件独立性得以保留，并让交叉项在求平均后抵消。

From scores to entropy

5. Fisher Information and Event Probabilities

Relating Fisher information to local event probabilities

Equations (158)–(165)

5.1 Why move to event probabilities?

We have separated the machines’ contributions.

The next question is:

How much Fisher information can one machine contribute through the transcript?

The proof bounds its contribution using the probability of its local event.

5.2 Equation (158) · From raw scores to the transcript score

∇θlog⁡pθ(T∣R=r)=𝔼θ[∑i=1nAi|T,R=r]=∑i=1nai(T).

The transcript score on the left equals the sum of the conditional block-score means on the right.

In words:

Though the server cannot see raw samples, the transcript’s sensitivity to the parameter is the conditional mean of their total score.

5.3 Equation (159) · Separate Fisher information by machine

tr⁡JT|R=r(θ)=𝔼θ[∥∑i=1nai(T)∥2|R=r]=∑i=1n𝔼θ[∥ai(T)∥2∣R=r].

This is where the rectangle property enters the information calculation.

We started with:

𝔼∥∑iai(T)∥2

Expanding the square introduces cross terms.

By (157) they cancel on averaging.

Only the individual squared terms survive.

This avoids an unnecessary factor of n.

5.4 Equation (160) · Bound each machine by its event probability

∥ai(t)∥2≤4vqiℓi(t)+2b2ℓi(t)2.

Apply Lemma 1 from III-B.

The first term is:

4vqiℓi(t)

Its three factors are:

  • v: concentration scale of a single-sample score.

  • qi: the machine’s number of independent observations.

  • ℓi(t): surprisal of its local event.

A rarer event permits a larger conditional score displacement, within this bound.

The second term is:

2b2ℓi(t)2

It accounts for the more general Bernstein tails.

5.5 Equations (161)–(162) · Where does entropy come from?

Substitute (160) into (159) and use qi≤q:

tr⁡JT|R=r(θ)≤4vq𝔼θ[∑iℓi(T)|R=r]+2b2𝔼θ[∑iℓi(T)2|R=r].

For the first term, (152) gives:

𝔼θ[∑iℓi(T)|R=r]=𝔼θ[ln1p(T)|R=r]=Hθ(T∣R=r).

Alice passes with probability 0.2 and Bob with probability 0.3. Their product is 0.06.

Compute the entropy. Fix the seed and suppose two transcripts have probabilities 3/4 and 1/4. Their surprisals are ln⁡4 for the rarer and ln⁡(4/3) for the more common. Weight by occurrence to get H=(3/4)ln⁡(4/3)+(1/4)ln⁡4≈0.562 nats. Looking only at the rare event would give ln⁡4.

Why?

Because:

∑iℓi(t)=ln⁡1p(t)

By the definition of Shannon entropy:

H(T)=∑tp(t)ln⁡1p(t)

Taking the expectation over transcripts gives entropy.

Local event probabilities give transcript surprisal; its mean is transcript entropy.

5.6 Equations (163)–(165) · Control the quadratic term

Since each ℓi(t)≥0:

∑iℓi(t)2≤(∑iℓi(t))2=ln⁡21p(t).

Consequently:

tr⁡JT|R=r(θ)≤4vqHθ(T∣R=r)+2b2𝔼θ[ln⁡21p(T)|R=r].

With at most B bits, there are at most 2B complete transcripts.

Lemma 2 bounds the last second moment:

tr⁡JT|R=r(θ)≤4vqHθ(T∣R=r)+2b2KB.

The Bernstein correction is paid once for the whole transcript.

Section recap

The first subsection separated the machines’ contributions.

The second establishes the following:

Each contribution depends on its local event probability, and the combined surprisals yield transcript entropy.

因为：

∑iℓi(t)=ln⁡1p(t)

而熵的定义恰好是：

H(T)=∑tp(t)ln⁡1p(t)

所以对所有记录求期望，就自动出现了熵。

这是 III-C 证明中最漂亮的转化：局部事件概率 → 整条记录的自信息 → 通信熵。

由于最多传输 B bits，完整记录至多有 2B 种。

由 Lemma 2，可以控制最后的二阶矩：

tr⁡JT|R=r(θ)≤4vqHθ(T∣R=r)+2b2KB.

注意：这意味着 Bernstein 修正项是对整个通信记录计算一次，而不是每台机器各自支付一次。

tr⁡JG,T(θ)=tr⁡JG(θ)+𝔼G∼Pθ⊗rtr⁡JT|G(θ;G).

Alice alone has observations 1 and 2; both have 3; Bob alone has 4 and 5.

What can the message add? Suppose G∼N(θ,1) and send one bit T=𝟏{G>0}. Knowing G=1.2 determines T=1; knowing G=−0.4 determines T=0. For any given G the transcript is deterministic, so its conditional Fisher information is zero. The chain rule gives JG,T=JG+0=1.

This is the chain rule for Fisher information.

Its interpretation is:

total information=information in shared data+additional transcript information given shared data

The joint score splits into marginal and conditional scores. Their cross term has mean zero, giving the additive decomposition.

IX(θ) denotes the Fisher information matrix of one raw observation.

  • Five distinct shared observations give five units.

Repeated access creates no additional independent sample.

2.6 Equations (200)–(202) · Cancellation of the localization scale

Define the dimensionless variable:

x=mDd

Why this normalization?

d/m is the typical mean squared error scale of a single machine with m Gaussian observations.

Hence:

x=Dd/m

This is the actual error relative to the local statistical scale.

At x=1 the error equals the reference scale; at x<1 it is smaller.

Define the localization surplus:

s=Bd−12log⁡2m,s+=max⁡{s,0}

Here:

  • B=nk: total communication in bits.

  • d: parameter dimension.

  • 12log⁡2m: localization cost per coordinate.

  • s: budget beyond that cost.

For instance, if d=1,m=256,B=10:

s=10−12log⁡2256=10−4=6

After subtracting the four-bit localization scale associated with local precision, six bits remain.

Now examine the cancellation.

By (30) and (200):

Bdln⁡2=sln⁡2+12ln⁡m

Bdln⁡2=sln⁡2+12ln⁡m,ln⁡(D/d)=ln⁡x−ln⁡m.

hTd≤sln⁡2+12ln⁡m⏟positive term+cπ+12ln⁡x−12ln⁡m⏟negative term

The two terms involving 12ln⁡m cancel.

We obtain:

hTd≤sln⁡2+cπ+12ln⁡x.

Five bits per coordinate minus the three-bit localization scale leaves two bits of surplus.

Check with numbers. Take d=1,m=16,B=4,D=1/32, giving s=4−12log⁡216=2 and x=mD/d=1/2. The budget and error terms of (198) sum to 4ln⁡2+12ln⁡(1/32)=1.5ln⁡2. Equation (202) writes the same sum as 2ln⁡2+12ln⁡(1/2)=1.5ln⁡2. Add back the same constant cπ in both forms.

This is the algebra worth showing on the board.

Starting from total budget B, subtract the localization scale to identify the surplus s governing refinement.

4. Removing the Quadratic Communication Correction

Remove the quadratic communication term.

Equations (213)–(220)

4.1 Which term must disappear?

We obtained:

D≳dm(1+s+)+r‾+(B+1)2

The desired expression is:

dm(1+s+)+r‾

The former denominator has the extra term:

(B+1)2

It comes from the Bernstein tail bound on scores.

Dropping a positive denominator term strengthens the lower bound and therefore requires proof.

The earlier risk bounds provide the needed argument.

4.2 Why split into m≥n2 and m<n2?

Consider two regimes.

When m is large enough, absorb the correction into the denominator.

For smaller m, invoke the accessible-sample lower bound from III-A.

Case A： m≥n2

Since:

B=nk

It follows that:

(B+1)2≤(k+1)2m

The correction is at most a fixed multiple of m.

Consequently:

m(1+s+)+r‾+(B+1)2≤[1+(k+1)2][m(1+s+)+r‾].

With k fixed, the additional factor is a constant.

Equation (212) now implies the desired (213).

With sufficiently large local blocks the correction does not affect the order of the bound.

Case B： m<n2

Use:

s=nkd−12log⁡2m

Since m<n2:

s=nkd−12log⁡2m≥nkd−log⁡2n.

For fixed positive d,k and sufficiently large n:

s≥nk2d>0,n≥n0.

Consequently:

[m(1+s+)+r‾]−1≤(ms)−1≤2dk(nm)−1≲(nm)−1.

What does the inequality say?

The refinement term is at most a fixed multiple of 1/(nm).

III-A already gives the accessible-sample lower bound:

RBB≳dnm

That sample-size bound implies the desired conclusion even without (212).

This regime uses a different inequality to complete the proof.

Combining the cases:

RBB(N,m,n)≳dm(1+s+)+r‾

Here RBB is the minimax risk over admissible blackboard protocols.

The risk constraint applies to every admissible protocol.

Pool size, total accesses, coding precision and refinement each give a bound. Take the largest.

c>0 is a constant independent of N,m,n; ℒ is the lower envelope defined in II-C.

5.3 Equations (223)–(227) · Rearrange the inequality

This time define:

t:=dmD.

This normalization differs from (205) in the general model.

From (222):

t≤r‾m+2hTd+4π2m

Using the entropy budget:

2hTd≤2Bdln⁡2−2(ln⁡2−1)+ln⁡(2πeD/d)=2sln⁡2+2−2ln⁡2+ln⁡(2πe)−ln⁡t.

t+ln⁡t≤2sln⁡2+r‾m+ln⁡(πe3/2)+4π2m.

The problem becomes:

How does an upper bound on t+ln⁡t yield an upper bound on t?

5.4 Equations (228)–(229) · The Lambert W function

Denote the right side of (227) by z.

Then:

t+ln⁡t≤z

Exponentiate:

tet≤ez.

We need to invert:

y=tet

A logarithm alone cannot isolate t, since t occurs both outside and inside the exponential.

Define the Lambert W function:

W0(y)eW0(y)=y,y>0.

Work through the inversion. Suppose the right side of (227) is z=2+ln⁡2. Exponentiation gives tet≤2e2. Substituting t=2 gives equality. Since tet is increasing on positive inputs, t≤2. Substitute t=d/(mD) to obtain D≥d/(2m). Equivalently, W0(2e2)=2.

It is the inverse of f(t)=tet on the positive real axis.

For example:

W0(e)=1

Because:

1⋅e1=e

Consequently:

tet≤ez⇒t≤W0(ez)

Substitute t=d/(mD) back in:

D≥dmW0((πe3/2)exp⁡[2sln⁡2+r‾/m+4π2/m]).

This is a nonasymptotic Bayesian risk lower bound.

It retains explicit constants without hiding a factor in c.

Since it holds for every admissible Gaussian protocol and estimator, it also lower-bounds Gaussian minimax risk.

Where does Lambert W fit into the proof?

It provides the final algebraic inversion.

The statistical argument comes from three earlier steps:

  1. Use the rectangle property for interactive communication.

  2. Separate the shared information as an additive contribution.

  3. Combine Fisher information with the transcript entropy budget.

Lambert W makes the final Gaussian bound explicit.

y=tet

把 t=d/(mD) 代回：

  3. 将 Fisher Information 上界与通信熵预算结合。

Lambert W 只是让最后的高斯结论具有显式常数。
